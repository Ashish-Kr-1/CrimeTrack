Core Design Assumption: FCSA is engineered to investigate suspects when you do NOT have their physical phone in custody. All tracking, location estimation, and network profiling are calculated using network-side carrier Call Detail Records (CDR) alone.

1. System User Roles & Separation of Duties

FCSA segregates system administration from active investigations to maintain audit compliance and casework integrity.

User Role Primary Responsibilities
Admin (System Administrator) • Carrier Normalizer: Configures operator templates so Jio, Airtel, Vi, and BSNL CDR formats map automatically.
• AI Engine Trainer: Calibrates and trains the ML coordinate prediction weights for suspect location forecasting.
• Security Auditor: Inspects system-wide tamper-proof access logs to audit analyst casework.
Analyst (Cyber Cell Investigator) • Log Ingester: Uploads cell tower logs (CDR) into active cases.
• Spatiotemporal Analyzer: Traces target tower-hopping trails, active hours, and hotspots on interactive maps.
• OSINT Investigator: Runs automated queries for WhatsApp, Signal, and historical threat databases.
• Case Exporter: Drafts legal orders and generates unified case dossier summaries.

3. Analyst Dashboard Workspaces & Features

The Analyst Panel provides a full set of digital workspaces, covering every stage of a telecommunications investigation in plain, easy-to-understand steps:

  • Upload Center (Data Import): This is where you upload raw Excel or CSV call logs (CDRs) received from telecom companies. Because different operators (Jio, Airtel, Vi, etc.) use different names for their columns, this center automatically standardizes the columns. It maps fields like "calling number," "dialed number," and "tower location" into a single uniform format, saving investigators from manual clean-up work.
  • Dashboard (Overview Screen): Provides a quick summary of the case. It automatically calculates an overall "Suspicion Score" for the suspect, labels their threat profile (e.g., flagging if they behave like a "Money Mule" account), and shows simple graphs of call and SMS volumes so you can spot unusual activity spikes.
  • Chrono Replay (Movement Map): An interactive map that visually replays the suspect's movements step-by-step. Investigators can hit a "Play" button, adjust playback speeds, and watch the target move between cell towers over time, showing exactly where they traveled and who they called at each location.
  • Risk Center (Rule Violations): A detailed ledger of security checks. It lists exactly which forensic rules the target has triggered—such as receiving OTP alerts from multiple banks or suddenly stopping all voice calls—explaining the specific behaviors that flagged them as suspicious.
  • Mobility Tracker (Habits Analyzer): Groups call data by time of day to profile the target's daily routine. It identifies their "Home Base" by clustering towers they connect to overnight (10:00 PM to 6:00 AM) and flags their "Operational Hideouts" by tracking daytime tower connections.
  • Geo Intelligence (Location Heatmaps): Displays maps showing where the suspect spends the most time. It automatically checks if the suspect has roamed across states or spent time in known cybercrime circle hotspots (like Jamtara), alerting investigators to high-risk locations.
  • Network Analysis (Ringleader Link Graph): Builds a visual connection chart linking the suspect's phone number to contacted numbers, handset hardware IDs (IMEIs), linked bank accounts, and cell towers. This makes it easy to trace co-conspirators and identify the leader of a network.
  • Active Cases (Case Manager): A digital filing cabinet. It allows analysts to organize multiple active cases, keep logs, save targets' contact files, and switch between investigations easily.
  • OSINT & Automation (Live Checks Control): A central dashboard where you can check the connection status of external databases (like WhatsApp and MISP) and toggle automatic alerts on or off.

4. Detailed Explanation of Cyber Features

FCSA automates 7 core cyber forensics features directly from the suspect's phone number, searching external public data networks and local platforms:

  • 1. OSINT (Open Source Intelligence): Scans public internet records and directories using the suspect's phone number to harvest digital footprints. It searches for links to external social media profiles, email addresses, and online names associated with the target.
  • 2. Wassenger (WhatsApp Status Tracker): Queries WhatsApp systems to see if the suspect's number is registered. It identifies if the account is a Personal or Business profile, retrieves their profile photo, and registers their active status.
  • 3. Signal (Encrypted App Check): Checks if the suspect has registered their number on Signal Messenger. Because secure encrypted apps are often used by advanced threat actors to hide their messages, finding an active Signal account helps profile the suspect's tech-savviness.
  • 4. Carrier Info (Operator & Circle Details): Verifies the SIM card's network provider (Jio, Airtel, Vi) and identifies the geographic region (telecom circle) where the SIM card was originally activated, ensuring the carrier information is correct.
  • 5. MISP (Blacklist Lookup): Cross-references the suspect's number against a database of blacklisted numbers reported in past lottery scams, phishing campaigns, and bank frauds, immediately flagging historical offenders.
  • 6. Splunk (Activity Auditing): Automatically records every search, click, and export made by the investigator on the system. This creates a secure, permanent audit trail to ensure legal compliance.
  • 7. Slack (Instant Warnings): Sends immediate notification cards to the Cyber Cell's Slack channels when a target's suspicion score crosses a critical danger threshold, alerting field agents instantly.

5. Detailed Explanation of AI Features

To track organized crime cells efficiently, FCSA integrates advanced machine learning models and heuristic indicators:

  • Cell Tower CGI Trajectory Mapping: Extracts latitude and longitude coordinates from tower logs to draw a visual travel route of the suspect's journey chronologically on the map.
  • ML Location Forecasting: An AI assistant that studies the suspect's past movements to predict their next likely location sector. This helps law enforcement position physical teams in advance.
  • Impossible Velocity (SIM Cloning Alert): Calculates the physical travel speed between consecutive calls. If a suspect's SIM connects to a tower in Delhi and then to a tower in Mumbai just 10 minutes later, the system flags a "SIM Cloning Warning" because traveling that fast is physically impossible.
  • Money Mule Heuristics: Automatically flags bank-fraud cards. It raises an alarm if a SIM card acts as an alert aggregator receiving SMS notifications from 5 or more different banks (indicating they are managing multiple stolen accounts) or spams UPI registration gateways.
  • Spatiotemporal Hotspot Clustering: An algorithm that groups location coordinates by time. It automatically identifies the suspect's home (where they spend nights) and hideouts (where they spend afternoons) by calculating the center-points of call locations.

6. Legal Request & Report Generator

FCSA speeds up legal paperwork and case reporting by automating document exports:

  • Section 91 CrPC Notice Drafts: Automatically drafts standard police legal request letters based on the suspect's logs, including requests to freeze bank accounts, block hardware IMEIs on national networks, or request UPI transfer logs from payment companies.
  • One-Click PDF Dossier Exporter: Compiles all maps, location heatmaps, timelines, threat rules, audit logs, and drafted legal notices into a single, clean PDF report ready to present in court or to senior officers.
